On-Page SEO Checklist: 10 Fixes for Every Page

An on-page SEO checklist is a list of the things you control inside each page that help search engines understand it, such as the title tag, meta description, headings, URL, images and links. Running through the same checklist on every page is the fastest way to improve your rankings. Here are ten fixes to apply to every page you publish.

What is the ideal title tag?

The title tag is the clickable headline shown in search results. Keep it under about 60 characters so it is not cut off, put the main keyword near the start and make it clear and specific. Every page needs its own unique title; never reuse the same one across pages.

A good example is 'On-Page SEO Checklist: 10 Fixes for Every Page'. It is short, contains the keyword and promises something concrete.

How should you write a meta description?

A meta description is the short summary shown under the title in search results. Write about 150 to 160 characters, include the main keyword once and describe the benefit of clicking. It does not directly raise rankings, but a strong description increases the number of people who click your result.

Make each meta description unique and written for humans. Avoid generic lines such as 'Welcome to my website'.

What makes a good URL?

A good URL is short, lowercase, uses hyphens between words and includes the main keyword, for example /on-page-seo-checklist. Avoid spaces, capital letters, random numbers and long strings of symbols. Every page must have its own unique URL, and once a page is live you should avoid changing it.

How should you use headings?

Use exactly one H1 per page for the main title, then H2 subheadings for the major sections and H3 for details beneath them. Write H2s as questions where possible, such as 'What is a meta description?', and answer them directly in the first sentence below. This question and answer format helps with answer engine optimization, because AI assistants and Google snippets prefer clear, direct answers.

How do you optimize images?

Images should be small in file size, ideally under 200 KB, so pages load fast. Compress them, give them descriptive file names such as keyword-research.svg instead of image1.png, and always add alt text that describes what the image shows. Alt text helps visually impaired users and lets search engines understand the picture.

What is good anchor text for links?

Anchor text is the clickable words of a link. Use descriptive text that tells readers where the link goes, such as 'our keyword research guide', instead of vague phrases like 'click here' or 'read more'. Link to related pages on your own site to help visitors and crawlers move around, and make sure every link has anchor text.

What else should you check before publishing?

Before you publish, confirm that the page has one main keyword and two related keywords, that the content fully answers the question, that paragraphs are short, and that the page works on mobile. Then submit or refresh your sitemap in Google Search Console.

If you are new to SEO, start with our beginner's guide to SEO first and come back to this checklist when you publish your next page.

How should you write image alt text?

Alt text should describe what the image shows in one short sentence, such as 'Checklist document with ticked items illustrating an on-page SEO checklist'. Include the keyword only when it fits naturally. Do not start with 'image of', do not stuff keywords and leave alt text empty only for purely decorative images.

What is internal linking and how many links should a page have?

Internal linking means linking from one of your pages to another using descriptive anchor text. It helps visitors find related content and helps crawlers discover and understand your pages. Two to five relevant internal links in a long article is a healthy range. Link only when the other page genuinely helps the reader.

Does mobile friendliness matter for on-page SEO?

Yes. Google mainly uses the mobile version of your page to index and rank it, so text must be readable without zooming, buttons easy to tap and nothing should scroll sideways. Test your pages on a phone before you publish. See our Core Web Vitals guide for related speed checks.

How often should you audit your on-page SEO?

Audit your most important pages every three to six months. Check that titles and descriptions are still unique, links are not broken, images are small and the content is still accurate. Update the publishing date only when you have made a real improvement.
