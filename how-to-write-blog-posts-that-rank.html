How to Write Blog Posts That Rank on Google

Writing a blog post that ranks on Google means choosing a keyword people search for, answering the question clearly and organizing the page so both readers and search engines understand it. You do not need to be a professional writer to do this. Follow this step-by-step method to turn any topic into a helpful, well-structured post.

How do you choose a blog topic?

Choose a topic that your audience actually searches for and that you can explain well. Start with keyword research, pick a long tail keyword and check the first page of Google to see what kind of content is ranking. If every result is a list, a list post is the safest format.

What is the best blog post structure?

A reliable blog post structure has five parts: a title containing the main keyword, a short introduction that gives the answer immediately, several H2 subheadings that each answer one question, short paragraphs with examples, and a conclusion with a next step.

Starting with the answer is important. Readers decide within seconds whether to stay, and Google often pulls the opening lines of a section into featured snippets.

How long should a blog post be?

There is no magic word count. A post should be as long as it takes to fully answer the question. For most beginner topics that means roughly 600 to 1,500 words. Thin posts of a few lines rarely rank, while padding with filler hurts readers. Cover the topic completely and stop.

How do you write headings and paragraphs for SEO?

Write H2 headings as questions people would actually ask, such as 'How long does SEO take?', and answer each in the first sentence below. Keep paragraphs to two or four sentences, use simple words and add bullet lists when you have steps or options. This question and answer style is also ideal for answer engine optimization.

Include the main keyword in the title, first paragraph and at least one heading, and use the two related keywords in other sections. Never force them.

How do you add images and links?

Add at least one original or properly licensed image with a descriptive alt text and a small file size. Link to related posts using descriptive anchor text, for example our on-page SEO checklist, and to trusted outside sources when you cite a fact. Avoid anchor text such as 'click here'.

How do you edit and publish a post?

Read the post aloud, cut anything that does not help the reader and check facts. Confirm the title, meta description and URL are unique, then publish and submit the page in Google Search Console. Revisit the post every few months and update outdated information. If you want AI help with drafting, see our favorite free AI tools.

How do you write a strong introduction?

A strong introduction answers the main question in the first one or two sentences, then tells the reader what the rest of the post covers. Avoid long warm-ups. For example, a post about SEO timing should open with 'SEO usually takes several weeks to a few months to show results' and then explain why.

How do you write a good blog title?

A good title is clear, specific and contains the main keyword near the start. Keep it under 60 characters so it is not cut off in results. Numbers, brackets and a promise of what the reader will gain, such as 'Step-by-Step Guide' or '10 Fixes', improve click-through rates.

What is E-E-A-T and how does it affect writing?

E-E-A-T stands for experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trustworthiness. It is the quality framework Google's reviewers use. Show it by sharing first-hand examples, explaining who wrote the post, citing reliable sources and keeping information accurate. Even a student can show experience by describing a real project step by step.

How often should you publish new blog posts?

Consistency matters more than volume. One good, complete post every week is better than five thin posts in a day. Choose a schedule you can sustain, such as two posts per month, and use your keyword map to plan the next topics.

What are the most common blog writing mistakes?

The most common mistakes are writing without checking search intent, burying the answer at the bottom, using long walls of text, copying other websites, stuffing keywords and forgetting to update old posts. Each one makes it harder for readers and search engines to trust your page.

Avoid them by planning with a keyword map, answering the question first, using short paragraphs and reading every post once more before publishing. A final pass with the on-page SEO checklist catches most issues.
