How to Invest in an IPO: A Beginner's Guide for India

An IPO, or initial public offering, is when a private company sells its shares to the public for the first time and lists on a stock exchange. If you want to know how to invest in an IPO, you need a demat account, a UPI linked bank account and an understanding of the price band, lot size and allotment process. This guide explains each step and the risks.

What is an IPO?

An IPO is the first time a company offers its shares to the general public. The company raises money, and early owners may sell some of their holding. After the issue closes, the shares are listed and start trading on the NSE and BSE like any other stock.

IPOs are part of the primary market, where new shares are created and sold. After listing, trading moves to the secondary market, as explained in our guide to how the stock market works.

What do you need to apply for an IPO?

You need three things: a demat account, a trading account and a bank account with UPI. If you do not have them yet, follow our guide on how to open a demat account.

Retail investors can apply for up to ₹2 lakh in one IPO. The money is not spent when you apply. It is only blocked in your bank account until the allotment result.

What are the price band and lot size?

The price band is the range in which you can bid, for example ₹95 to ₹100 per share. The lot size is the minimum number of shares you must apply for, such as 150 shares. Your minimum investment is the lot size multiplied by the upper end of the price band.

Bidding at the cut-off price means you accept whatever final price the company decides within the band. This gives the best chance of allotment.

How do you apply for an IPO step by step?

Applying takes only a few minutes in your broker's app:

  • Open the IPO section while the issue is live. Most issues stay open for three working days.
  • Choose the IPO, enter the lot quantity and the bid price, and enter your UPI ID.
  • Approve the mandate request in your UPI app to block the money.
  • Wait for the allotment, which is decided a few days after the issue closes.

How does IPO allotment work?

If the IPO is subscribed less than 1x, everyone gets their shares. If it is oversubscribed, retail applicants are chosen by a lottery, so you may get nothing even if you applied correctly. Applying through several family members' accounts, each with their own demat account, increases the chances.

After allotment, unused money is unblocked in your bank account and allotted shares appear in your demat account. The shares then list on the exchange within about three working days after the issue closes.

How do you research an IPO before applying?

Do not apply just because everyone is talking about it. Read the offer document, called the RHP, and check these points:

  • What the company does and how it earns money.
  • Three to five years of sales, profit and debt, using fundamental analysis.
  • How the money raised will be used, and whether it is a fresh issue or an offer for sale by existing owners.
  • The valuation compared with listed competitors.
  • The risk factors section of the document.

Be careful about grey market premium. It is an unofficial number and it is not reliable.

What is the difference between a fresh issue and an offer for sale?

In a fresh issue the company creates new shares and the money goes into the business. In an offer for sale (OFS) existing shareholders, such as promoters or early investors, sell some of their shares and the money goes to them, not to the company.

A healthy IPO often has a good share of fresh issue used for growth or debt repayment. A pure OFS is not bad, but ask why the owners want to sell.

Should you hold or sell an IPO share on listing day?

It depends on why you applied. If you applied for a quick listing gain, set a target and a stop-loss before the day starts and stick to them. If you applied because you believe in the business, ignore the first day noise and judge the company by its results over the next few quarters.

Do not decide on emotion when you see the opening price. Use the same discipline as for any other trade, as explained in our guide to technical analysis basics.

What are the risks of IPO investing?

IPO shares can list below the issue price, and a good listing day does not mean the share will keep rising. Many new companies are valued highly because of excitement. Decide your holding period before you apply: either you invest for the long term or you accept the risk of a short term listing trade.

Keep each IPO bet small and use the same risk management rules as for any other investment. This article is for education and is not investment advice.
