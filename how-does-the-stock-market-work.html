How Does the Stock Market Work? A Beginner's Guide

The stock market is a marketplace where people buy and sell small ownership pieces of companies, called shares or stocks. Prices rise and fall every second because buyers and sellers keep agreeing on new prices through a stock exchange such as the NSE or BSE. This guide explains how the stock market works in plain language, from the first share to your first order.

What is a stock and what does owning one mean?

A stock is a small unit of ownership in a company. If a company has one crore shares and you own one thousand of them, you own a tiny slice of that business and share in its success. Companies sell shares to raise money for growth, and investors buy them hoping the business will become more valuable.

Owning a share can pay you in two ways. The share price may rise, which gives you a capital gain when you sell. The company may also pay part of its profit to shareholders as a dividend. Neither is guaranteed, and the price can fall as easily as it can rise.

What is a stock exchange and how do the NSE and BSE work?

A stock exchange is a regulated platform that matches people who want to buy shares with people who want to sell them. In India the two main exchanges are the National Stock Exchange (NSE) and the Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE). You never walk into an exchange. Your broker sends your order to it electronically in a fraction of a second.

The exchange does not set the price. It only provides the system where buyers and sellers meet. The market regulator, the Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI), writes the rules and watches for unfair practices so that ordinary investors are protected.

How are share prices decided?

Share prices are decided by demand and supply. If more people want to buy a share than sell it, buyers compete and the price moves up. If more people want to sell, the price moves down. The price you see on screen is simply the last price at which a buyer and a seller agreed to trade.

Every order sits in an order book. Bids are the prices buyers will pay and asks are the prices sellers want. When a bid and an ask match, a trade happens. Because thousands of orders arrive each second, the price keeps changing all day.

Why do stock prices go up and down?

Prices move because people change their opinion about what a company is worth. Common triggers include:

  • Company results: higher profits usually lift the price, weak results pull it down.
  • News and events: a new product, a big contract, a court case or a change in management.
  • The economy: interest rates, inflation, oil prices and government policy.
  • Global cues: moves in US and Asian markets often affect Indian shares the next morning.
  • Sentiment: fear and greed can push prices beyond what the business fundamentals justify.

To stop extreme swings, exchanges apply price bands, often 5%, 10% or 20% a day, beyond which a share cannot move until the next session.

What are the stock market timings and how does settlement work?

The Indian stock market is open Monday to Friday from 9:15 am to 3:30 pm, with a short pre-open session before it starts. It is closed on weekends and on exchange holidays.

Settlement means the shares and the money actually change hands. India follows T+1 settlement, so if you buy a share on Monday it reaches your demat account on Tuesday. To hold shares you need a trading account and a demat account, which we explain in our guide on how to open a demat account.

How do beginners place their first order?

You place an order through a broker's app or website. The two basic order types are a market order, which buys or sells immediately at the best available price, and a limit order, which only trades at the price you set or better. Beginners usually prefer limit orders because they give control over the price.

Start with a small amount you can afford to leave invested for years. Pick a company you understand, check its numbers with fundamental analysis, and learn to read a price chart using our guide to how to read stock charts.

What is the difference between the primary and secondary market?

In the primary market a company sells new shares directly to investors, for example through an IPO, and the money goes to the company. In the secondary market investors trade existing shares among themselves on the NSE and BSE, and the money goes to the seller, not the company.

Almost everything you see in daily market news happens in the secondary market. You can learn how the primary market works in our guide on how to invest in an IPO.

What is the difference between investing and trading?

Investing means buying shares to hold for years and benefit from business growth. Trading means buying and selling over days, hours or minutes to profit from short price moves. Investing needs patience and research into the company, while trading needs strict discipline and risk control.

Beginners usually do better by investing first. Trading is harder than it looks because costs, taxes and emotions eat into small gains.

What are the risks of investing in the stock market?

The main risk is that you can lose some or all of the money you invest. Share prices can fall for months, and a company can even go out of business. Money you will need within a year or two should not be in stocks.

You can reduce risk by spreading money across several companies, investing regularly and never borrowing to buy shares. Our guide to risk management in stock trading shows how to protect your capital. This article is for education only and is not investment advice.
