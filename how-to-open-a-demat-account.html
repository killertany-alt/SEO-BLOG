How to Open a Demat Account Online: Step-by-Step

A demat account is an online account that holds your shares in electronic form, just like a bank account holds money. To buy shares in India you need a demat account, a trading account and a linked bank account. This guide shows how to open a demat account online, which documents you need and what to check before choosing a broker.

What is a demat account?

A demat account, short for dematerialised account, stores your shares, bonds and mutual fund units digitally instead of as paper certificates. When you buy a share, it is credited to your demat account, and when you sell it, the share is debited. Two depositories, NSDL and CDSL, keep these records, and a broker acts as your depository participant.

Your demat account is where your ownership is recorded, so it is safer and faster than paper. You can see your holdings on your phone at any time.

What is the difference between a demat account and a trading account?

A trading account is used to place buy and sell orders on the exchange, while a demat account stores the shares you own. You need both to invest. The trading account sends the order, the demat account holds the result, and your bank account provides or receives the money.

Most brokers open all three links together in one online application, so you rarely have to handle them separately. Before trading, it helps to understand how the stock market works.

Which documents do you need to open a demat account?

You need a few documents, and nearly all can be uploaded from your phone:

  • PAN card, which is mandatory for market accounts.
  • Aadhaar number with a mobile number linked to it for OTP based e-KYC.
  • Bank proof such as a cancelled cheque or a recent bank statement.
  • A passport size photo, a photo of your signature and sometimes income proof if you plan to trade derivatives.

Make sure the name on your PAN, Aadhaar and bank account match. Mismatches are the most common reason for a rejected application.

How do you open a demat account online step by step?

Opening a demat account online usually takes 10 to 20 minutes of work plus a day or two for approval. The process is the same at most brokers:

  • Choose a SEBI registered broker and click the open account option.
  • Enter your mobile number and email and verify them with OTPs.
  • Complete e-KYC with PAN and Aadhaar, then upload your photo, signature and bank details.
  • Add a nominee, sign the application with an Aadhaar OTP and wait for approval.
  • Receive your login details, add money through UPI and place your first order.

How do you choose the right broker?

Compare brokers on charges, platform quality, customer support and trust. Check that the broker is registered with SEBI, which you can verify on the SEBI website. Avoid anyone who promises guaranteed returns or asks you to hand over your login.

Look closely at four costs: the account opening fee, the annual maintenance charge, the brokerage on each trade and the small depository fee charged when you sell shares. Discount brokers charge low flat fees, while full service brokers charge more but add research and advice.

What are the charges for a demat account?

Many brokers open a demat account free, but an annual maintenance charge may apply. Every trade also carries brokerage, exchange charges, GST and government taxes such as the securities transaction tax. These are small on each trade, yet they add up if you trade very often.

Read the broker's charge sheet before you sign up so there are no surprises, and compare it with at least one other broker.

How long does it take to open a demat account?

Most online applications are approved within one to three working days once your documents are verified. Delays usually happen when the signature is unclear, when the name differs between PAN and Aadhaar or when the bank details do not match.

If the application is rejected, the broker will tell you the reason. Fix the mistake and resubmit instead of opening a new account elsewhere.

Can you have more than one demat account?

Yes. You can hold more than one demat account, even with different brokers, and each one is separate. Some investors keep one account for long term holdings and another for active trading.

More accounts mean more charges and more passwords to protect, so open a second one only if you have a clear reason. For tracking what you own, check your consolidated account statement.

How do you keep your demat account safe?

Never share your password, OTP or PIN with anyone, including people who claim to be from your broker. Turn on two factor authentication, use a strong password and check your holdings and contract notes regularly.

Add a nominee so your shares can pass to a family member without long delays. Once your account is ready, you can learn what to buy with our beginner guides on fundamental analysis and SIP vs lump sum investing.
