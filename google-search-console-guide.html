Google Search Console Guide: Setup and Sitemap

Google Search Console is a free tool from Google that shows how your website appears in search, which pages are indexed, which queries bring visitors and which errors need fixing. Every website owner should set it up on day one. This guide walks through adding your site, verifying ownership, submitting your sitemap and reading the key reports.

What is Google Search Console and why use it?

Google Search Console is a free service that reports on your site's presence in Google Search. It tells you whether Google can crawl and index your pages, which searches show your site, how many clicks and impressions you receive and whether anything is broken. Without it you are guessing.

How do you add your website to Google Search Console?

Sign in at search.google.com/search-console, click Add property and choose URL prefix. Paste your full website address, including https, and click Continue. URL prefix is simplest for beginners because it supports easy verification methods and matches exactly the address you enter.

How do you verify website ownership?

Google needs proof that the site is yours. The easiest method for a static site is the HTML file method: download the verification file Google gives you, upload it to the root of your website next to index.html, redeploy, and click Verify. Alternatively, copy the HTML tag and paste it inside the head section of your homepage.

Leave the verification file or tag in place permanently, because removing it can un-verify your property.

How do you submit a sitemap?

A sitemap is a file, usually sitemap.xml, that lists the pages you want Google to find. After verification, open Sitemaps in the left menu, type sitemap.xml and click Submit. A status of Success and a count of discovered pages means Google read it correctly. Make sure your robots.txt file also points to the sitemap.

How do you ask Google to index a page?

Paste a full page address into the search bar at the top of Search Console to open URL Inspection, then click Request indexing. New properties have small daily quotas, so if you see 'Quota exceeded', try again the next day. Your sitemap still lets Google discover pages on its own; requesting indexing just speeds it up.

Which reports should beginners check first?

Check the Pages report to see which URLs are indexed and why others are not, the Sitemaps report for submission status and the Performance report for clicks, impressions and the queries that find you. Expect data to appear after several days. Use what you learn to improve titles and content with our on-page SEO checklist, and review the basics of SEO if any term is unfamiliar.

What is the difference between a URL prefix and a domain property?

A URL prefix property covers only the exact address you enter, including the https and www version. A domain property covers every subdomain and protocol under a domain but needs DNS verification, which is harder on free hosting. For a .vercel.app site, use the URL prefix property.

What does 'Discovered – currently not indexed' mean?

It means Google knows the page exists, usually from your sitemap, but has not crawled it yet. This is common for new websites. Keep your sitemap submitted, add internal links to the page, improve the content and wait. Requesting indexing may speed it up.

What does the Performance report show?

The Performance report shows four numbers: total clicks, total impressions, average click-through rate and average position. You can filter by page, query, country and device. Look for queries with many impressions but few clicks; improving those titles and descriptions is a fast win.

How often should you check Search Console?

Check it once or twice a week while your site is new. Look for indexing errors, new queries and any messages from Google. After each major update, such as publishing new pages, resubmit your sitemap and inspect the key pages.

What problems can Search Console help you fix?

Search Console flags problems such as pages that cannot be crawled, pages blocked by robots.txt, missing or broken sitemaps, mobile usability issues and slow Core Web Vitals. Each report explains the issue and lists the affected pages, so you know exactly what to fix.

After you fix a problem, use the Validate fix button so Google rechecks it. Use the on-page SEO checklist to prevent the same issues on new pages.
