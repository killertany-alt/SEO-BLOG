Nifty 50 and Sensex Explained for Beginners

The Nifty 50 is the main index of the National Stock Exchange and the Sensex is the main index of the Bombay Stock Exchange. When the news says the market is up, it usually means one of these two numbers rose. This guide explains what a stock market index is, how the Nifty 50 and Sensex are built and how you can use them.

What is a stock market index?

A stock market index is a number that tracks the combined performance of a group of shares. Instead of checking thousands of companies, you watch one number that shows whether the market as a whole is rising or falling.

An index is calculated from the prices of its member companies. When most members rise, the index rises. Think of it as a report card for a section of the market.

What is the Nifty 50?

The Nifty 50 is the benchmark index of the NSE and it tracks 50 large, actively traded Indian companies from sectors such as banking, information technology, energy and consumer goods. It is the most watched indicator of the Indian stock market.

The Nifty 50 is reviewed regularly, and companies can be added or removed if they no longer meet the rules on size and trading activity.

What is the Sensex?

The Sensex is the benchmark index of the BSE and it tracks 30 large and well established companies. The name comes from the words sensitive and index. Because it has fewer members, it is a slightly narrower view than the Nifty 50.

In practice the Nifty 50 and the Sensex move almost together, since many of the same big companies sit in both.

How are index values calculated?

Both indexes use free float market capitalisation. Market capitalisation is the share price multiplied by the number of shares. Free float counts only the shares that the public can actually trade, leaving out the promoters' locked holdings.

A company with a larger free float market value has a larger weight, so a 2% move in a big bank shifts the index more than a 2% move in a small company. This is why a handful of heavy companies can drive the index on a given day.

Why do the Nifty 50 and Sensex matter to you?

These indexes matter for three reasons:

  • Benchmark: you can compare your portfolio's return with the index to see whether you are doing well.
  • Sentiment: a sharp fall across the index often signals fear in the whole market.
  • Investing: index funds and ETFs copy these indexes, giving you a low cost way to own many companies at once.

Understanding the market starts with knowing how the stock market works.

What are other important indexes?

Besides the headline indexes, there are many others. Bank Nifty tracks major banks. Nifty Midcap and Nifty Smallcap track medium and smaller companies. Sector indexes cover areas such as IT, pharma and auto.

Mid and small company indexes can rise faster but also fall harder, so they carry more risk than the Nifty 50.

What does it mean when the Nifty 50 hits a record high?

A record high means the index closed at its highest level ever. It shows that large companies together are worth more than before, but it does not mean every share is rising or that a fall is coming.

Markets spend a lot of time near highs during long bull runs. Avoid deciding to buy or sell only because of a headline. Your plan and time horizon matter more.

What is the difference between a price index and a total return index?

A price index tracks only share prices. A total return index also adds the dividends paid by the companies, so it shows the true gain an investor would have received. Over long periods the total return is noticeably higher than the price index alone.

When you compare your fund with the market, compare it with the total return version to be fair.

How can beginners invest in the Nifty 50 or Sensex?

You cannot buy an index directly, but you can buy a fund that copies it. An index fund and an ETF both hold the same shares in the same weights as the index. Many beginners start a monthly plan in such a fund, as explained in our guide to SIP vs lump sum investing.

You will need a demat account for ETFs. Read how to open a demat account to get started. Past index returns do not guarantee future results.
